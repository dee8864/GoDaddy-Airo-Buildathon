## The Full Story (From Idea to Live App)

### The Spark: A Real Problem
As a student at **GL Bajaj Institute of Technology & Management**, I noticed two things happening simultaneously:
1. Students are always looking for affordable food, salon services, stationery, and hangout spots.
2. Local shopkeepers near the campus have great offers but struggle to broadcast them to the student community.

The gap? **No centralized, hyperlocal platform** to bridge the two. Students rely on word‑of‑mouth or random WhatsApp forwards, while merchants rely on outdated paper flyers.

### The Opportunity
One day, our placement department (headed by **Sunil Chourasia sir**) sent a mail about the **GoDaddy Airo Buildathon** – a 3‑hour individual challenge to build and launch a live web app using GoDaddy’s AI builder. The prize? A super dream package of 44 Lac, 1‑year free Airo subscription, and a feature on the GoDaddy podcast.

There was just one catch: **I had zero prior knowledge of web development or coding.**

### The Strategy
Instead of seeing this as a limitation, I saw it as a challenge. I decided to compete by focusing on:
- **Product Thinking** – solving a real, tangible problem.
- **AI Prompt Engineering** – using GoDaddy Airo AI Builder to translate my ideas into a functional app.
- **User‑Centric Design** – ensuring the interface was intuitive and visually appealing.

I brainstormed multiple ideas (skill‑swap, lost‑and‑found, event ticket management) but finally settled on the **Local Coupon & Deals Aggregator** – because it had the highest daily utility for my campus.

### The Build (3 Hours – No Code)
I named it **CampusSaver** – a name that instantly conveys value.

1. **Defining the Core Features:**  
   I listed the must‑haves: deal listings, category filters, merchant submission forms, and a way to get directions.

2. **Crafting the Perfect Prompt:**  
   Instead of generic instructions, I gave GoDaddy Airo a structured prompt:
   > *“Create a web app called CampusSaver with two sections – a grid of deal cards showing image, business name, discount %, and expiry countdown; and a merchant form to submit deals with name, phone, address, price, and image.”*

3. **Iterative Refinement:**  
   After the initial generation, I went back and added:
   - **Category filters** (Food, Fashion, Salon, Stationery, Electronics, Gym).
   - A **“Open in Google Maps” button** using free deep‑linking (`https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=ADDRESS`) – zero cost, zero API key.
   - Styled it with a vibrant orange‑and‑white theme to energize student users.

4. **Launch:**  
   With 30 minutes to spare, I tested the flow, added sample deals from real local shops, and published it live.

### The Outcome
I walked away with a fully functional, mobile‑responsive web app that solves a genuine daily problem. More importantly, I proved that **product thinking beats coding skills** when you have the right AI tools.

## Key Features

- **Deal Discovery:** Browse deals by category (Food, Fashion, Salon, Stationery, Electronics, Gym).
- **Detailed Deal Cards:** Each card shows image, shop name, discount percentage, expiry date, and a quick glimpse of the offer.
- **Merchant Submission Form:** Local businesses can submit their deals directly (name, address, phone, image, price, terms).
- **Zero‑Cost Navigation:** The “Open in Google Maps” button takes users directly to the shop’s location via the Google Maps app/website – no embedded maps, no API costs.
- **Expiry Awareness:** Deals are marked with expiry dates to ensure relevance (planned auto‑hide feature).
- **Mobile‑First Design:** Optimized for students browsing on their phones.
